# Spike — THE ASSAYER, procedural, in Godot 4.7, animated through its cycle

**The question this spike exists to answer.** `TRAILER.md` §7 lists *"the Assayer has never
been modelled in Godot"* as the hardest gap in the announcement trailer and says plainly that
if shots 23–25 cannot be built, **Act V drops**. It is also the game's only antagonist that
is not another player and the object the whole fiction hangs on (`THE-MACHINERY.md` §7). So:

> Can the Assayer be generated by rules — no imported mesh, no bitmap, no UV — at true scale,
> driven through its full seventy-five seconds by one clock, and lit by nothing but itself?

**Answer.** Yes for all four. The machine is **20,336 triangles** built by twenty-six rules,
none of which opens a file; the cycle is
one function of one float; and the only lights in the chamber are its own two hot points and
whatever a visitor carries. Whether it reads as *ancient* rather than as *a crane* is the part
I am least sure of and I have written what I think below rather than claiming it.

Nothing outside `spikes/godot/assayer/` was written. `spikes/godot/cave/` was read and copied
from, never modified.

**What "procedural" means here, exactly** (`DESIGN-PRINCIPLES` §5 and §6). Nothing in this
directory imports a mesh, opens an image file, or unwraps a UV. Every vertex is produced by
GDScript at load; every material is world-space or model-space; the only `load()` calls in the
whole spike are for the five `.gdshader` files. The four `sampler3D`s are noise volumes
**generated by `FastNoiseLite` at startup**, which is `PROCEDURAL-AND-GODOT` Q5 and its default
answer of *yes, allowed* — the same call `DESIGN-PRINCIPLES` §6 flags as needing a ruling. If
that ruling goes the other way, this spike loses its rock and its casting skin and keeps
everything else.

## 2. Every part, and the rule that generates it

At **0.6 m per cell** (`ART-DIRECTION` §5.1, inherited and stated nowhere in the repo).
`THE-MACHINERY` §2.1 gives the cell dimensions; §5.1 gives the same numbers in metres. Where
a part is in neither, its source is named.

### 2.1 The casting vocabulary — five rules, and everything obeys them

`ART-DIRECTION` §5.2: *"Every part is a casting, and that is a geometry rule a generator can
execute: fillets at every junction, webs and ribs on every flat plane, 2° draft, a visible
mould parting line, and raised part numbers and a foundry mark."* Those are the five
functions in `mb.gd`, and the machine is built by calling them:

| rule | function | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| draft | `prism`, `prism_ring`, `tbox` | every prism narrows toward its top; every member tapers along its length. Two degrees over a 1.8 m mast section is 63 mm, which is what makes a 6 m column read as **cast** rather than **extruded** |
| ribs and webs | `web` | a triangular gusset extruded `t` thick, at every junction that carries load. Without it a tapered box reads as a stick |
| bolted, never welded | `bolt` | a hex head on a washer, standing proud. A mast this tall was shipped in sections and the bolt lines are the evidence |
| the parting line | a 4 mm `prism_ring` at mid-height | the cheapest possible "this was poured", and it is visible under a lamp at two metres |
| raised marks | `lettering` | strokes on a 3×5 unit grid, 13–26 mm proud, cast into the flat. No date anywhere on the machine |

### 2.3 The chamber, and the scour

`chamber.gd` is deliberately minimal — enough rock to hold a 6.6 m machine, take its light and
give it one passage to be seen from. Radius 10.5 m, crown 11.4 m, drive mouth 2.4 × 2.4 m
(`CONCEPT 11`, and `AD` 5.5's rule that *"a chamber that holds something worth seeing must be
taller than the passages that reach it"*). The aperture is cut by **not emitting the quads the
mouth occupies**; the drive's own tube covers the gap.

**The scour** is the only thing added to the cave's rock material, and it is added as
`AD` 5.3 asks rather than as `TM` 2.1 asks: *display tints the scour; the world de-silts it.*
One radial factor about the machine at exactly the 9-cell lethal contour, its edge displaced
by a metre-scale noise so the transition happens over 1.6 m and wanders by another 1.4 m —
**there is no circle anywhere in it.** It drives five things at once: `mud` (the compacted
muck that buries the aggregate everywhere else), `fines`, albedo (+78%, bare fresh-fractured
rock is *paler* than the floor round it), roughness, and the joint frequency (×3, because
freshly-broken ground breaks smaller). The scatter obeys it too: angular spall inside at a
quarter the count, rounded rubble outside, and one muck pile against the far wall, because
`DESIGN-PRINCIPLES` §7 says every object has to answer who put it there.

**The last firing's lobe** is a fractionally paler wedge, ±40° about the aim from 1.9 to 5.4 m,
`alb *= 1 + lb·0.13`. Never a glow, never magenta, no boundary. `CONCEPT 10` says it may be
too subtle and that this is a knob; it is `lobe_amt` and it is currently 1.0.

## 3. The cycle, its timings, and their source

**One clock.** `cycle_t` is the only thing in the scene that moves. The aim, the hammer's
height, the chain's length, the tank's level, the colour and output of both hot points, the
recoil and the drip are all pure functions of it, computed in `Assayer.set_phase()`. There is
no `AnimationPlayer`, no second timeline, and no per-frame geometry rebuild anywhere: the
machine is three static meshes under three transforms (the fixed body, the **turret** which
rotates, the **hammer** which translates), plus two bars scaled on one axis, plus two
emissives and eight lights whose colour and energy are set.

| p (s) | state | what happens | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 – 54 | **listening** | nothing moves. One drip every two seconds onto the anvil. The residual glow of the last strike is still decaying for the first 4.15 s | `TM` §3, `AD` 5.4 |
| 54 – 57 | **the slew** | the turret eases 36° and stops. The bright race is the tell | `TM` §3, `ANCIENT_SLEW_S 3.0` |
| 57 – 62 | **locked** | five seconds of stillness after a movement | `TM` §3, `ANCIENT_LOCK_S 5.0` |
| 62 – 71 | **the wind** | nine clicks at 1 Hz. Click *n* at p = 61+*n*. Each click is a **step**, not a ramp: 120 ms of travel and then it is held. The hammer rises 3.5/9 m, the tank falls one ninth, the winch head goes 1900 K → 2400 K | `TM` §3, `CONCEPT 7` |
| 71.00 – 71.15 | **the fire** | the hammer falls the whole travel, quadratically in time because it is falling free. 4000 K at 4× | `TM` §3 |
| 71.15 – 71.65 | **the recoil** | the whole rig jolts down 0.4 cells and recoils, damped, over 0.5 s | `TM` §3 |
| 71 – 79.15 | **the decay** | output falls as (1−d)^2.4 over 8 s and the colour falls with it, because a cooling body does both at once. This crosses the cycle boundary, so the machine is never quite instantly dark | `AD` 5.4 |

**The aim** starts at `ANCIENT_AIM_0_DEG = 41.5` and steps `−36°` per cycle, always the same
direction — learnable from two slews.

**One number was changed from the doc, deliberately, and it is the wind's output curve.**
`AD` 5.4 says *"ramping 0 → full in nine discrete one-second steps"*. Built literally, click 1
puts **0.18%** of the frame above the legibility threshold: it is invisible, and an invisible
first click cannot be counted. `TM` §3 already fixes the right law for the same nine seconds
on the acoustic side — *"strength 0.3 → 1.0 — exactly what `ancient.signature_strength()`
already returns, and now it is the winch taking load"* — and the light is the same winch
taking the same load. So `out = 0.30 + 0.70·n/9`. Measured: click 1 goes 0.18% → 4.2% legible.
**The colour curve is unchanged** and is the concept renders' own: 1900 + 500·n/9 K.

## 4. The light, and the numbers behind it

**Two hot points, not one, and this is `AD` 5.4's measured half.** A source at the mast head
is a **beacon** — visible from the next chamber, which is its job — and it does not light the
ground the agents fight on, so a second goes at the anvil block at the mast foot at about a
third the output. Both are point sources *beside* their emitters, and both emissives and both
point sources are driven from the same two numbers, so the band never glows without lighting
anything and never lights without glowing.

**The colour is computed, not picked.** `AssayerMaterials.blackbody()` is the standard
piecewise fit to the Planckian locus, **linearised** before it reaches a light or an emission,
because Godot uses `Color` values raw and an sRGB-encoded 1900 K amber arrives with 55% green
instead of 26% — pale orange instead of ember.

**The energy was calibrated twice, and the first table was measuring a bug.** Same frame
(click 9, 8.5 m, 52° vertical) at six energies, read against `AD` 2.9's **linearised** bands:

| energy | > 0.05 legible | < 0.02 black | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 1.5% | 94.5% | unplayable |
| 45 | 2.7% | 90.1% | under the 3% floor |
| **65** | **4.9%** | **79.8%** | inside the contract |
| 90 | 8.5% | 65.5% | loses the ≥70% black |
| 125 | 16.0% | 55.7% | a lit level |
| 170 | 29.1% | 49.5% | — |

**72** is what shipped. It is a ratio and not a wattage; `AD` 2.2 is explicit that it does not
survive a change of renderer.

**Falloff is 2.0 — inverse square** — which is what `AD` 2.3's *"do not compress the ramp"*
means for a point source. Godot defaults an omni to 1.0, which is 1/d and flat enough to light
a whole chamber; 2.4 was tried and is steeper than physics.

**No ambient, no fill, no rim, no sky term, no bounce card.** `env.ambient_light_source` is
`DISABLED` and the background is black. Volumetric fog at 0.0105 with anisotropy 0.55 and
albedo (0.85, 0.86, 0.90), which is `AD` 2.6's resting silt — **the glow in the air at the
strike is that silt, not a bloom.** Glow threshold 1.35 with `glow_bloom` 0.03, so nothing but
the two hot points ever crosses it.

### 4.1 Three lighting bugs that were each worth finding

1. **Both sources were sealed inside their own castings.** The winch light sat 0.10 m from the
   axis of a 0.29 m capped drum with two 0.36 m flanges. Everything in the room was in its
   shadow. It presented as shadow acne, and 0.06 / 0.14 / 0.30 bias were all swept and were
   **identical to five decimal places** — which is what said it was occlusion, not acne. `AD`
   5.4's *"point sources beside the emitters, not inside the mast"* is not a stylistic note.

2. **The chamber floor plate roofed over the drive.** A 22 × 22 m plate lifted 0.55 m outside
   r = 11.1 m to "meet the wall" put a step of rock across a 2.4 m drive mouth. The passage
   frame rendered at **0.00000 mean luminance** and read as a shadow problem for four sweeps,
   because the plate only blocks when shadows are on. The shell's own bottom row is undisplaced
   at exactly r = R, y = 0, so the floor meets the wall by construction and needs no ramp.

3. **The anvil's index mark was on a surface no light in the world can reach.** Both sources
   are on the machine's axis, so **every vertical face of the anvil has N·L between −0.14 and
   −0.92** — it is turned away from everything the fiction supplies. That is why the mark is
   now cast **twice**: once on a 34° chamfer, which the winch head 5.7 m up rakes at N·L 0.44,
   and once on the flat below, which is for a visitor's work lamp. Foundries cast marks on more
   than one face for the same reason.

### 4.2 The exposure contract

`python lumcheck.py` asserts `AD` 2.9 on **linearised** luminance (the doc is explicit that
three of four earlier passes computed it on gamma-encoded values and were wrong by a large
factor). The `clear_` frames are reported without a verdict, because the CLEAR rig is the
concept board's studio and is not the game — a bright studio elevation must never become the
reason the contract gets relaxed.

Four in-situ frames sit outside the contract **and should**, and they are named here rather
than quietly tuned into it:

- `11_insitu_click1` (1.2% legible): the machine at 38% of its output. The 3% floor is written
  for a **lamp** frame; the first notch of a wind is legitimately dimmer than that.
- `17_insitu_from_the_passage` (0.8% legible, 97.6% black): the only lit thing in it is a
  2.4 m arch five metres away. That is the frame.
- `21_insitu_wide_dormant` (0.2% legible): a dormant Assayer **emits nothing**. What you are
  measuring is a work lamp nine metres from a black object, which is the dormant read.
- `14_insitu_strike`: over 20% legible, which `AD` 2.9 explicitly allows — *"up to 50% for a
  shaft or a strike"* — and under the 3% blown ceiling. `lumcheck.py` applies the lamp-frame
  rule to it and is wrong to; the verdict, not the frame, is what needs amending.

## 5. What I took from the concept art, what I changed, and why

**Taken as given, without re-inventing:** the hub is the anvil (1); the mechanism dressing —
ratchet track, winch drum, brake band, water motor, chain, sheave, pawl housing, bolted
flanges, ladder, stays, mass box (3); the sight glass (4); the tank at 3 m on a bracket with a
rising main going up into the dark (5); the two-mark index system with no dates (6); the winch
and anvil as the two emitters with click *n* at 1900 + 500·*n*/9 K (7); the eight-stanchion
platform with four of eight bays left (8); the scour as material rather than tint (9); the lobe
as a pale decal in the silt (10); the 10.5 m chamber under an ~11.5 m crown (11); the download
play-bow with the lamp **off** (15).

**Changed, and each with a reason:**

| change | why |
|---|---|
| The **counterweight, boom and stays sit on a turret at 5.40 m and the mast continues to 6.60 m** carrying the winch head | This is concept design-problem 1 answered. `TM` 2.1 lets a ring riding the mast climb *through* the boom's height, which no physical build can do. The concept's answer — the slew ring at 5.40 and a 3.5 m drop — is taken, and the mast is kept at its full 11 cells so the beacon is where `AD` 5.4 measured it. **81 kJ becomes ~56 kJ.** Still squarely a seismic weight drop. |
| The **wind's output ramp is 0.30 → 1.00**, not 0 → 1 | §3 above. Measured. |
| The **rib ends are transducer cans in bearing steel**, not a `HAZARD` band | `AD` 5.2 forbids `HAZARD` as a material on this object; only the field is magenta. The ends are the things that touch the rock. |
| The **index mark is cast twice**, and on the two flats that look out through a *missing* grating bay | §4.1 (3). A surviving bay is a lid over the mark. |
| The **anvil has a chamfer** | The only surface on the block a mark can be cast on and still be read in the machine's own light. |
| The **anvil emitter is six shadowless points on a 0.62 m ring**, not one | A ring has no single shadow, and a point at its centre stands inside the mast and blacks out the floor it exists to light. |
| **Cast iron is at `rust = 1.55`**, not the cave's 1.00 | `kit.gdshader` scales corrosion by `lowness`, because in the cave nothing manufactured is more than ~2.5 m up. This machine is 6.6 m tall and at 1.00 everything above 3 m came back as clean grey metal — the one thing `AD` 5.2 forbids. |
| **The chamber rock's fracture is 0.12**, not the cave's typical 0.52 | At 0.52 the cellular joint sits at 0.7 m, and a 10 m wall lit by one point source 6 m away reads as **brickwork**, because the pattern tiles and a point source shows the whole tile at once. In the cave the same number is right, because nothing there is ever lit at that range by anything but a 27° spot. |
| **No standing water**; the datum is at −0.30 m and the footing pads below 0.34 m are graphitised with a mineral crust band above them | *"The water was here and it has gone down"* is what a drained working looks like, and it gets `AD` 5.2's third age of iron into the frame without putting a puddle under a scour whose whole claim is that nothing settles here. |
| **A ladder missing three of its bottom six rungs, and one grating bay lying on the floor** | Not in any document. A ladder with every rung is maintenance; a ladder that has lost its wet end is a century. A platform somebody dismantled and a platform that fell down are different objects. |

## 6. What THE-MACHINERY does not specify and I had to decide

Everything on this list is a **guess**. It is the list `CLAUDE.md` requires.

1. **The turret.** That the boom, counterweight and stays ride a 0.65 m sleeve on the ring
   while the mast head above it does not turn. The doc says the boom is at z = 9 and the mast
   goes to 11; it never says what happens between them.
2. **The hammer's drop is 3.50 m** and therefore ~56 kJ rather than 81. See §5. This is a
   design decision, not an art one, and it is the one I most want a ruling on.
3. **The ratchet pitch, 97.2 mm** — four teeth per click over 3.5 m in nine clicks.
4. **Where the winch head is**: drum axis along X on a bracket on the +Z side of the head at
   y 6.30, with the brake band as the emitter and the water motor opposite it.
5. **That a click is a step and not a ramp**: 120 ms of travel, then held. The doc says "nine
   clicks, one per second" and nothing about the shape of one.
6. **The recoil's shape**: a damped cosine over 0.5 s. The doc gives the 0.4-cell depth and the
   0.5 s duration and nothing else.
7. **The decay's shape**: `(1−d)^2.4` with the colour falling as `sqrt(d)`. `AD` 5.4 gives
   "decay over 8 s as the brake cools" and no curve.
8. **The tank's fill law.** This is concept design-problem 3, built as the docs jointly imply
   rather than papered over: the tank draws down one ninth per click across the nine seconds of
   the wind and refills linearly over the other sixty-six. So **the sight glass is rising for
   88% of the cycle** — a second, slow countdown a viewer can read from the floor, and the
   reason the period is seventy-five seconds. Whether that is wanted needs a ruling.
9. **All eight guy anchors, the tie rods and the turnbuckles.** The doc says "bolted into the
   bedrock"; four guys at 45° is how a mast is actually held.
10. **Every dimension of the winch drum, brake band, water motor, sheave, pawl housing, chain,
    lightening holes, guide shoes, lifting ears, bearer sizes, rivet counts and bolt counts.**
11. **The glyph shapes** in `mb.gd`'s `_GLYPH`. Legible as an index, unreadable as language.
12. **The chamber**: profile, drive length, the 62° dog-leg, the muck pile, the scatter counts,
    and that the crown is 11.4 m.
13. **The Surveyor's entire form** beyond its 0.77 m length and 0.57 m standing height. It is a
    placeholder and `TRAILER` §7 already says the machines are.

## 8. Does it read as a machine, or as a prop?

My honest judgement, having looked at every frame.

**It reads as a machine, and specifically as a machine somebody built for a purpose.** The
things that carry it, in order of how much work they do:

1. **The mechanism is complete and connected.** The chain leaves the drum, turns the sheave,
   comes down the +X flat and ends at a shackle on the pawl housing; the pawl bites a ratchet
   track whose teeth are the only polished thing on the mast; the tank feeds the motor that
   turns the drum and drips on the anvil the hammer lands on. Nothing is a detail that goes
   nowhere. A viewer does not have to be told what it does.
2. **The ratchet track and the bright race.** `AD` 5.2's claim that *"a viewer reads it is
   still running off the shine on the bearing before anything moves"* is true in these frames,
   and it is the single strongest thing in the material scheme.
3. **The two hot points, working as the doc predicted.** The winch head is a beacon that lifts
   the top of the frame and lights almost no floor; the anvil ring lights the ground agents
   stand on. In the in-situ frames the mast between them is lit by nothing else, exactly as
   5.4 asks.
4. **The evidence of people.** The ladder, the service platform, the handrails, the bolt lines,
   the lifting eye. A machine with a ladder was serviced by somebody who is not here.

**What still reads as a crane rather than as ancient**, and I would fix these next:

- **The silhouette is a derrick.** Mast, boom, counterweight, guys — the outline says
  *lifting appliance*, and nothing in the outline says *this hits the ground*. The hammer is
  the part that would say it, and at 1.20 m across on a 0.96 m mast it is only 25% wider than
  what it rides. **If one thing gets another day, it is the hammer's silhouette.**

  What was tried, and what it taught: the hammer's mark is its **bearing-steel top flange**,
  because the only light during the wind is 1.7 m above its top of travel, so the top flange
  is the one surface on the moving part squarely facing the source. That works — and it works
  *only from above*. From an eye at 0.40 m the flange is edge-on for the whole nine seconds
  and the hammer reads as a thickening of the mast. `17_clicks_1_5_9_strip` is therefore shot
  from a rig at 3.40 m, where clicks 1 and 9 are unmistakable and click 5 is arguable. **A
  countable countdown currently depends on a camera height**, and that is a real finding
  rather than a framing preference: the mark that survives every eye height is the light
  itself (the ratchet track and the anvil ring both step visibly across the three panels) and
  the tank level, not the hammer's position. `TM` 2.4 already predicted the shape of this —
  *"the hammer climbing the mast is the redundant mark, deliberately"* — and this build is
  evidence that it is redundant in 3D for a different reason than it was in the schematic.
- **The surfaces are too even at range.** `kit.gdshader` is doing real work up close, but at
  8 m the iron is one dark value. The cave spike's answer — the wear field responding to what
  happened *there* — has nothing to respond to on a machine with no traffic. Flowstone at the
  drip point was cut from `AD` §9 for being a hand-modelled asset; as a displacement shader
  driven by a downward-flow and curvature mask it is the missing thing.
- **The chamber is a smooth room.** It has enough relief not to be a dome and not enough to be
  a stope. That is a chamber problem and not a machine problem, but it is what the machine is
  seen against.

**And the honest one:** these frames were composed by the person who built the object. Every
"it reads" above is a claim about my own eye. The gate is a human looking at shot 24 and saying
whether they can count to nine.

## 9. The trailer's three shots

All four sequences are in `shots/seq/`, at 1280×720, one PNG per frame, captured at a fixed
timestep (`TRAILER` §6 — *"capture at a fixed timestep and render offline rather than dropping
frames on screen"*). Every move obeys §8's camera rules: a dolly of under a metre, eased in and
out on a cubic, never linear, and every eye is at floor level (0.40 m) or machine height, never
hovering.

| sequence | frames | phase | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `s23_mast_in_the_dark` | 24 | 47.0 → 50.4 | **Shot 23.** A mast resolving out of a visitor's own beam, 9 m off, not moving. It emits nothing; the only reason it is visible is the lamp. |
| `s24a_the_slew` | 26 | 53.7 → 57.3 | **Shot 24, first half.** The boom swings 36° and stops, pointing. The bright race is the only thing that catches. |
| `s24b_the_wind` | 38 | 61.7 → 71.02 | **Shot 24, second half.** Nine clicks, the winch head hotter and brighter at each, the hammer climbing, the tank falling. |
| `s25_the_strike` | 27 | 70.90 → 73.10 | **Shot 25.** The strike, the recoil, and two seconds of decay. |

**Shot 24 is two shots and this build says so.** The trailer gives it four seconds to cover
p 54 → 71, which is seventeen seconds of machine. There is a cut in there whatever anyone does,
and telling an editor that is more use than pretending one continuous take exists.

**Two things the sequences taught that the stills did not**, and both are about the two beats
where the machine emits nothing:

- **Shot 23 has to be taken from about five metres, not nine.** A dormant Assayer emits
  nothing at all, so the only thing that can find it is the visitor's own beam, and a beam
  falls off with the square of the distance. At 9 m the mast was under the legibility floor
  and the shot was a lit patch of floor with a black object behind it.
- **Shot 24's slew is only visible if the camera looks at the race.** `AD` 5.4 gives the slew
  *"nothing. The arm moves; the bearing race is bright metal and catches whatever is
  present."* That is exact: the lamp is a child of the camera and pitches with it, so a camera
  aimed at 4.6 m leaves the 5.4 m race outside the cone and the tell is invisible. Aimed at
  the race it is the brightest thing in the frame and it travels as the boom turns. **The slew
  is a shot the director has to know about**, which is `TM` §3's *"one director change, worth
  0.1 days"* arriving as a camera note rather than a rule change.

**Are they deliverable?** Yes, with one caveat named in `TM` §10.3 that this spike cannot
touch: *"six cycles is enough to teach the index; thirty seconds is not."* Shot 24 shows nine
countable clicks. It does not show that the aim indexes 36° every time, because that needs two
firings and 150 seconds. The trailer does not have 150 seconds and should not try.

## 10. What this spike does not do

- **No audio.** The ratchet at 0:03 that pays off at 1:39 is `TRAILER` §5's best idea and it is
  not in here.
- **No post-processing beyond tonemap and a tight glow.** `TRAILER` §9's list — depth of field,
  motion blur, grain, vignette, chromatic aberration — is unbuilt, and §9 is explicit that each
  must be measured on its own before it ships.
- **No provocation, no interfacing, no damage, no lobe as a hazard.** This is the *object*, not
  the mechanic. `TM` §5 and §6 need two agents and a policy.
- **The siblings.** No Haulage, no Bus.
- **The Surveyor is a placeholder** and its download pose is one hand-set pose, not an
  animation.
- **The scour and the lobe are drawn from the machine's own transform**, which is fine for a
  spike and is exactly the class of thing that must be re-derived from world truth, never from
  a policy, when this reaches the client. `CLAUDE.md`'s one invariant is not at risk here —
  nothing in this directory has a `Belief` — but the seam is worth naming before it exists.

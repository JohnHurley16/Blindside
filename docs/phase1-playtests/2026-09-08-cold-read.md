# Cold read — 2026-09-08

**A rehearsal of the Phase 1 spectator gate. Not the gate.** P1X-22. The gate is a person
watching eight minutes with sound, motion and Recall in her hand
(`PHASE-1-SPECTATOR-TEST.md`, *Gate*). This was agents reading still pictures. Nothing here
passes or fails Phase 1, and nothing here is written as though it could.

---

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

Three agents were shown still frames from the current build with no context beyond "you cannot
drive it" and asked what they understood. **Two returned a read. The third returned an empty
record** — `{"viewer": 3}`, no fields. Whether it failed or declined is not recoverable from the
payload, so this is a two-viewer rehearsal and is scored as one. Nothing below is averaged over
a missing answer.

**Frames are harder than the video in three ways**, and each of them suppresses a real signal:

- **No motion.** Every beat this display was built around is a *change over time* — the tether
  lengthening, the ghost being flung, the cloud smearing and snapping, the beacon travelling
  from where it was to where it is now, the countdown hand sweeping, the camera easing between
  subjects. The phase spec's "single most important visual" is literally a motion event: *the
  point cloud visibly smears under drift and snaps on a fix.* A still frame cannot show any of
  it. Every complaint below that is really "nothing changed" has to be read against a format
  in which nothing can change.
- **No sound.** P1X-20 rebuilt the whole mixer to carry distance, ownership, approach, wind-up
  and death. The rehearsal saw none of it. The September tester's complaint was *"things kinda
  beep"*; whether that is fixed is exactly what this format cannot say.
- **Twenty-second sampling.** Nine consecutive near-identical frames across the middle third is
  weak evidence of dead air on its own. It happens to agree with P1X-2's tick-by-tick
  measurement, which is why it is taken seriously below — but the agreement is what makes it
  evidence, not the frames.

**Frames are easier than the video in two ways**, and both inflate the result:

- **Unlimited time per frame.** A viewer who can stare at a caption for as long as she likes is
  not the viewer the gate is about.
- **An agent reads every label; a bored person reads almost none.** Both readers said so
  themselves, unprompted and in nearly the same words. Viewer 1: *"a lot of what I understood, I
  understood because a label told me… Strip the text off this and I'd have a cave, a dot, and no
  idea."* Viewer 2: *"too much of the meaning arrives as text."*

**The consequence for weight, in both directions.** Where this format says something is
**legible**, treat it as a *ceiling*: the real number is at or below it, because a person will
read fewer words than an agent did. Where it says something is **absent** — no mark on screen for
an event, no key, no unit, no result — treat that as **strong**, especially for anything the
design says is persistent: a permanent mark that appears in none of 35 frames is not a sampling
artifact. And where it says something is **boring**, treat it as a hypothesis only. An agent
given unlimited time cannot be bored. "I stopped caring at 3:53" is a judgement about content,
not a state anyone was in.

### 1.1 What was actually shown, and what was left out

36 PNGs in `docs/phase1-playtests/cold-read/`. One is black (`0000.png`); 35 carry content. 25
are at 20-second spacing and 11 at 4-second spacing across 5:13–5:53, of which two duplicate the
wide series, so **34 distinct pictures**. Filename maps to the on-screen clock as
**filename minus 7 seconds**: `0240.png` is 2:33, `dense-0548.png` is 5:41, `0800.png` is 7:53.

Three problems with the sample itself, and they change how two of the readers' loudest complaints
should be read:

1. **The cold open was not sampled.** `phase1/view/cold_open.py` runs for 6.5 s before 0:00 and
   states seven lines: *no radio / you cannot drive it / the pale one is yours / the orange one is
   not / it must bring two loads home / in eight minutes / you can call it back once.* The first
   content frame is 0:13. Both readers asked what `PRESS R` does; the card answers it. Both
   readers worked out which machine was theirs from the rail rather than being told; the card
   tells them, in the machines' own colours. `--snap` takes negative times for exactly this
   purpose and was not used.
2. **The reveal was not sampled.** The match ends at 8:00 and `phase1/match/reveal.py` draws truth
   over belief for the following twelve seconds; `RENDERS.md` records the rendered duration as
   8:12. The last frame here is 7:53. Both readers ended with *"there is no result"* — and there
   is one, six seconds after the last picture they saw.
3. **No provenance.** The directory is untracked and no commit, seed or sensor is recorded.
   `RENDERS.md` exists precisely so that a render is reproducible; this rehearsal has no
   equivalent, so it cannot be re-run against a changed build as a clean A/B.

Items 1 and 2 are defects of the rehearsal, not of the build. They are listed again in §4 with
that label attached, because two of the readers' "never understood" entries dissolve if the
sample is taken properly.

## 2. Scored against the gate's four pass signals

`PHASE-1-SPECTATOR-TEST.md`: *"they talk to the screen, guess at contacts, form a wrong theory
and correct it, and report tension at the Recall decision."*

| # | Pass signal | Can this format test it? | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talks to the screen | **No.** | Unavailable. Both readers wrote at length, and that is worth nothing: they were asked to. Unprompted speech is the whole signal and there is no way to observe it in an agent that was prompted. Do not read the volume of these reads as engagement. |
| 2 | Guesses at contacts | **Partly.** | **Weak fail.** They guessed at *causes* — viewer 2 invented a collision to explain the 2:33 error jump — but neither ever guessed at a **contact**. | 3 | Forms a wrong theory and corrects it | **Yes.** | **Pass, once, cleanly.** Viewer 1 read the hollow white glyph and the solid one as two separate vehicles at 1:13 and 1:33, then corrected at 2:53 when the tether appeared with `32 cells` written on it: *"Before that line existed I had it wrong. does not get tested until someone's hand is on the key.

## 3. Against September

`2026-09-06-gate.md` is the only human data point. The comparison is not like-for-like — a person
on a video of the old display versus agents on stills of the new one — so it is drawn narrowly,
on the four things she actually said.

| What she said | Then | Now |
|---|---|---|
| *"it was hard to understand"* | Silent for eight minutes, understood nothing she could name | **Changed, decisively.** Both readers named the place, the objective, the deadline, the two machines, the ownership of each, the hazard, the death and the central mechanic. Viewer 1 had the premise *"in about one frame"*. |
| *"all that changes is things kinda beep and nothing really is obvious"* | No event was legible as an event | **Changed.** Both readers listed six named events with timestamps. Viewer 2 on the 2:33 readout: *"I got it immediately, without the labels, purely from the colour and size difference."* |
| *"it needs more action"* | — | **Not changed.** Both readers, independently: the only physical event in the match happens to the machine that is not yours. Viewer 2: *"the only real event in the match — a machine dying in gas — happens to the other one, and I have no stake in it. That's backwards."* |
| *"it would be easier to understand in 3D"* | 2D point cloud | The display is now 3D. **Neither reader mentioned dimension, depth, or spatial confusion once.** That is not proof the change worked, but the complaint that motivated it did not recur. |
| **When she disengaged: the middle** | the middle | **The middle.** Viewer 1: *"around 3:53, and I'd fully checked out by 4:33."* Viewer 2: *"around 4:00, definitively by 4:33."* |

**The finding that matters is the last row.** Three viewers, two builds apart, in two formats, one
of them human — all three stopped in the middle third, and the two who could give a time gave the
same time to within seven seconds. The display work moved the diagnosis from *illegible* to
*legible and inert*. Those are exactly the two failure modes `SPECTATOR-DISPLAY.md` §11 named —
*"mystery fails by being illegible… dread fails by being inert"* — and we have moved from the
first to the second, which is progress and is not a pass.

### 3.1 What all of them missed, including her

**The spoof.** The phase spec calls it *"the moment the test is built around"*. Nobody has ever
seen it. She said nothing. Viewer 1: *"I think it's going the wrong way because it doesn't know
where it is, but I'm inferring that; nothing on screen says so."* Viewer 2: *"This is the single
most important event in the video and the screen offers no cause… I invented a collision theory
from the two machines being nearby and I have no idea if it's right."*

Three viewers have now watched a rival player sabotage a machine's map and not one of them has
worked out that **another player did something to it**. Without that, the game reads as a robot
that malfunctions, which is a documentary, not a competitive game. §5 of `SPECTATOR-DISPLAY.md`
specifies the fix in detail — the beacon travelling from old position to new over 0.45 s, turning
`lie` yellow, and **leaving a permanent dashed line back to where the machine still records it,
labelled `THE RIVAL MOVED THIS`**. A permanent mark appears in none of the 35 frames from 2:33 to
7:53. Either it is not built, or it is drawn somewhere the camera never is.

**Two smaller shared misses.** Nobody read the belief inset — she could not read the old cloud
either. And nobody registered the hazard's seventy-five-second heartbeat as a rhythm, only as a
nuisance.

### 3.2 One thing this rehearsal settled that was open

`SPECTATOR-DISPLAY.md` §11 set a test for the picture-in-picture arrangement: *"If the viewer
never looks at the inset and still says 'it thinks it's over there and it isn't', the design has
worked and the inset is a bonus. If she never looks at the inset and cannot say that, the inset
was never the problem."*

**Both readers never used the inset and both said it anyway**, from the tether and the two
numbers. Viewer 2: *"The yellow line does its job better in one stroke."* By the document's own
test, the arrangement works and the inset is not carrying the argument. That also means the
inset's remaining defects (§4, D8) are cheap to leave alone and not worth a day.

## 5. What to do before a person is asked to sit down again

**Is it ready? No.** But nothing left on this list is a redesign, and the two biggest items are
decisions rather than builds. The scarce resource is a fresh non-engineer who has never seen it;
spending one on a build with a free key sitting unbound and no result frame would waste her.

Cheapest first:

1. **Re-sample the frames properly and record the provenance.** ~20 minutes. `--snap` with
   negative times for the cold open, and past 8:00 for the reveal; write the commit, seed and
   sensor down the way `RENDERS.md` does. Two of the readers' "never understood" entries may
   dissolve without a line of code changing, and until that is known those two are unproven.
2. **Render a viewing with Recall actually sent.** ~30 minutes, no code. It fires the one inert
   control (D3), gives the match a second act and an ending, and puts on screen the best beat the
   sim has produced by itself — `2026-09-07-standing-on-the-shaft.md`: the machine standing five
   cells from its own extraction point for three minutes, structurally unable to find out.
   Recall at 2:00 is the timing that extracts.
3. **Wire up `map_key.py` and give the timeline pips a legend.** Hours. The code is written.
4. **Give `cells` a referent.** Hours. A scale bar on the minimap, or restate the error in a unit
   a stranger owns — *"37 cells — ninety seconds' walk"*. The walking-time Dijkstra §5 already
   specifies for the point-of-no-return line is the same number.
5. **Stop cutting to empty hazard windows.** Hours, in `director.py`. Rule 10 has now cost two
   viewers the one hazard that mattered. Cut to a window only when something is in it or walking
   at it; teach the seventy-five-second cycle on the timeline instead, where it is free.
6. **Draw the spoof's author, persistently.** ~a day, and it is already specified in §5. Find out
   first whether it is built and merely off-camera; if so this is a director fix, not a drawing
   one. **This is the highest-value display change on the list.**
7. **The designer's two open decisions, and they are worth more than everything above.**
   **P1X-17** — should the player have a reason to go to the machinery? Nothing else on this list
   puts the protagonist at risk, and M1 is the loudest complaint from both readers.
   **P1X-25** — freeze versus drift: which do you want? M2 is the direct cause of both readers
   disengaging at 4:00, and the trade is measured and written up.
8. **Then P1X-18** (designer self-test on the live window, with sound, sending Recall), **then
   P1X-19** with someone who has not seen it.

One thing to decide before P1X-19: **the September tester has now seen it once.** A second session
with her is no longer a cold read on legibility, though it is still the cleanest available test of
whether the changes fixed what she named. If both are wanted, they are two people.

## 6. What this rehearsal cannot tell us

Stated plainly so that nothing here gets quoted later as though it were the gate.

**It cannot tell us whether anyone is bored.** An agent with unlimited time per frame is not
capable of the state the gate measures. Both "I stopped caring at 3:53" lines are content
judgements, and they are useful because they agree with P1X-2's independent measurement — not
because anyone was bored.

**It cannot tell us whether the sound works.** P1X-20 is entirely untested here, and P1X-20 was
built to answer the September tester's actual words. Two of the readers' complaints — *"the
picture never once threatened it"*, *"nothing on screen said why"* — are precisely the sort of
thing a directional wind-up tone might answer.

**It cannot tell us whether motion carries what stills cannot.** Every mechanism in this display
that was designed to *move* was invisible to this rehearsal: the smear and snap, the ghost being
flung, the beacon travelling, the ring closing, the camera easing, the swap. The acceptance
criterion *"point cloud visibly smears under drift and snaps on a fix"* is structurally
untestable in stills.

**It cannot tell us whether the middle third is really dead.** Nine near-identical frames at 20 s
spacing is a sampling artifact until something else corroborates it. Something else does, which
is why it is in §4 — but the rehearsal did not establish it.

**It cannot tell us whether Recall feels like anything.** No decision was in anyone's hands, and
none was even sent in the render.

**It cannot tell us whether anyone cares about the machine.** `SPECTATOR-DISPLAY.md` §10.1 is
blunt about this and it has not changed: attachment comes from authorship, authorship is Phase 2,
and no display buys it.

**Only a person can supply four things:** unprompted speech at the screen; the felt cost of eight
continuous minutes rather than 34 sampled instants; a reaction to sound; and a decision with a
clock running on it. Those are pass signals 1 and 4 in full and half of 2. **This rehearsal
covered signal 3 and part of signal 2, and nothing else.**

## 7. What was guessed

- **That the two readers are independent.** They were run separately and their reads differ in
  wording, emphasis and several judgements, but they are the same model family and their strong
  agreement should be discounted somewhat for that. Where they agree with the September tester
  or with an instrumented measurement, that discount does not apply.
- **The filename-to-clock mapping** (filename minus 7 s) is inferred from the clocks visible in
  `0200.png`, `0240.png`, `dense-0532.png` and `0800.png`, and from the two duplicate files. It
  is not recorded anywhere.
- **That the frames come from the current `HEAD` build**, on an unknown seed and sensor. Nothing
  records it. The run is not the seed-7 match in `SPECTATOR-DISPLAY.md` §5 — the rival dies at
  5:41 here and at 6:56 there — so the beat sheet cannot be used to fill in what falls between
  frames.
- **That the spoof's permanent mark is not drawn.** Established by its absence from 35 frames,
  which is strong for a mark specified as permanent and weaker if the camera simply never held
  it. Check the code before spending the day in §5.6.
- **The ordering of §4 by "cost to a viewer"** is a judgement. The evidence supports the set of
  defects and the two readers' own rankings of the worst few; the exact order below the top three
  is mine.

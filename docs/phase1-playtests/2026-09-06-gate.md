# Phase 1 gate playtest — 2026-09-06

**Result: fail.** Recorded per `PHASE-1-SPECTATOR-TEST.md`, which says a bored player is a
fail, to stop, and to report when they disengaged. This is R1 in the risk register — "the
run phase is boring; whole design collapses; unfixable later" — and Phase 1 exists to
answer it.

## Who and how

A non-engineer, the designer's fiancée, who had not seen the project before. The designer
self-tested first (BLD-15) and reported it green.

**What she watched: the rendered video, not the live window.** This matters and is recorded
here rather than argued away, because it makes one of the three pass criteria structurally
unavailable — see "The confound" below.

## What she said

Verbatim as relayed by the designer:

- She said nothing at all until the end.
- In the middle she was getting bored, "because there was no interaction going on and if
  there was it was hard to tell."
- "It would be easier to understand in 3D vs the 2D demo."
- At the end: it was hard to understand, and it needs more **action**. "It's hard in the
  demo to actually get excited because all that changes is things kinda beep and nothing
  really is obvious."

## Scored against the gate

| Pass criterion | Observed |
|---|---|
| Talks to the screen | **No.** Silent until the end. |
| Guesses at contacts | **No.** |
| Forms a wrong theory and corrects it | **No.** |
| Reports tension at the Recall decision | **Not available.** No Recall decision existed in what she watched. |

**When she disengaged:** the middle. Not "engaged for four minutes then drifted" — there is
no reported moment of engagement to drift from. The spec says that distinction decides the
diagnosis: drift is a pacing problem, never engaging is a design problem, and they are
fixed differently. On the evidence this is closer to the second, with the confound below
as the one reason to doubt it.

## The confound

She watched a recording. The gate says "watch a *player* for eight minutes", and the
tension criterion is about a decision — Recall, the single input the player has. A video
has no decision in it, so her "there was no interaction going on" is literally true of the
format rather than a judgement about the design. This was flagged before the session and
the session was run on video anyway.

That confound covers criterion 4 and part of the boredom. It does **not** cover "it was
hard to understand" or "nothing really is obvious": those are about whether the display
makes events legible, and a live window shows the same display.

## What this does not yet tell us

Her 3D suggestion is a hypothesis about the fix, not an observation. Phase 1 deliberately
renders the point cloud in 2D and the design intends 3D eventually, so it may be right —
but treating a possible design problem with a rendering change is the expensive way to be
wrong, and one bored viewer is not enough evidence to spend that.

The narrower reading of the same words: **the events are not legible as events.** Sounds
arrive as beeps with no visible consequence; the spoof, the drift, the rival's approach all
happen without anything on screen changing in a way a stranger can name. That is testable
much more cheaply than a dimension change.

## Consequence, per CLAUDE.md

Phase 2 does not start. It was built on 2026-09-06 to 07 on the reading that this gate had
passed; that reading was wrong, and the build does not change the gate. It stays where it
is, unadvanced, until Phase 1's gate is met.

## Recommended next step

Re-run the gate live before concluding, because the cheapest experiment that separates the
two hypotheses costs twenty minutes: `python -m phase1`, her hand on the Recall key, one
sentence of instruction ("you cannot drive it; you have one button"). If she is still bored
with a live decision in front of her, the diagnosis is design and R1 is real. If she leans
forward at Recall, it is legibility and pacing, and the fix is in the display.

Either way the observations above stand and the report is not rewritten to match a later,
happier session.

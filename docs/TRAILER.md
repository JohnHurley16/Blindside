# Announcement trailer — treatment and shot list

2026-09-09. Two minutes, cut to be shown cold to someone who has never heard of this game.
Everything in it is captured from something that runs: the three Godot spikes and the Phase 1
match. Nothing is mocked up, and no shot may be a still pretending to be footage.

---

## 1. What the trailer has to do

A trailer for a game about autonomy has one hard problem: **the thing that makes it special is a
thing the player does not do.** Every other game trailer sells agency — look what you control.
This one has to sell the opposite and make it feel like a gain rather than a limitation.

So the whole cut is built on one reversal, placed at 0:50:

> You spend the first act being shown a machine you are preparing and teaching, in daylight, with
> your hands on it. Then it goes down the shaft and you never touch it again, and the rest of the
> trailer is you watching.

Everything before the shaft is warm, lit, ordered and close. Everything after is dark, and you are
a spectator. If a viewer feels the moment control leaves, the trailer has worked.

**The one line the trailer exists to deliver**, and nothing may crowd it:

> **It only knows what it has seen.**

**The image nobody else has.** The cut between the real cave and what the machine believes — a
lamp-lit passage, then the same passage as concentric rings of returns with hard shadows behind
everything. That cut is the trailer's signature and it should happen three times, each time
meaning something different: first as a reveal, then as a discrepancy, then as a catastrophe.

**Tone.** Industrial, wet, patient, quiet. Not a power fantasy. The reference is a documentary
about a deep mine, not a sci-fi shooter. No voiceover. No music with a drop. Text cards are few,
short, and never explain a mechanic.

## 4. Text cards, in full

Five, and no more. Each on black or in the black part of frame, held long enough to read twice.

1. 0:24 — *Nothing down there answers to you.*
2. 0:46 — *You cannot drive it. You can only teach it.*
3. 1:01 — *Twenty seconds after it goes down, it stops listening.*
4. 1:15 — *It only knows what it has seen.*
5. 1:47 — *And it can be lied to.*

Typeface: the game's own. No animation beyond a slow fade. Never over a bright frame.

## 5. Sound

There is no score. The trailer is built from the game's own audio, which already exists in
`phase1/audio/` and is a real mixer rather than a library of samples.

- **Act I** — rain on steel, water in a pipe, the yard's own hum, one unexplained distant ratchet
  at 0:03 that is not explained until 1:39.
- **Act II** — close and dry. Servo, footfall on concrete, the panel's own tones.
- **Act III** — the descent is the whole sound design of the trailer. Surface noise recedes over
  five seconds until there is nothing but the machine, then a held silence under card 3.
- **Act IV** — footfall, water, the sweep of the sensor. The belief cut at 1:13 should be
  **silent for its first half second**, then the sweep comes in. Sound is what tells the viewer
  they have changed register, not a whoosh.
- **Act V** — the ratchet, nine clicks, rising in pitch and level, then the strike, then room
  tone. The fold at 1:45 gets no sound at all, which is worse.
- **Act VI** — rain, and nothing.

**The rule:** every loud moment is followed by a longer quiet one. The trailer should be
noticeably quieter than the ones it will be shown next to, and that is the point.

## 6. Rules for capture

- **In engine, in motion.** No stills. A held shot is a locked-off camera in a running scene, not
  a frozen frame.
- **Capture at 1920×1080 and 60 fps**, then conform. Where a project cannot sustain 60, capture at
  a fixed timestep and render offline rather than dropping frames on screen.
- **No debug overlays, no HUD, no frame counters, nothing with a filename in it.**
- **The exposure contract in `ART-DIRECTION.md` §2.9 holds.** No shot is brightened to make it
  read. If a shot is unreadable it is the wrong shot.
- **The belief shots follow `ART-DIRECTION.md` §8.2 as rewritten**: opaque points, ring structure
  intact, nothing drawn into a sensor shadow.
- Capture one project at a time. Two Godot processes on one GPU produced a 13:1 spread in an
  earlier measurement and will produce dropped frames here.

## 7. What is honestly missing, and how the cut handles it

Recorded here rather than discovered in the edit.

| Gap | How this cut works around it | Would fix it |
|---|---|---|
| Machines are placeholder shapes | Kept small in frame, backlit, in silhouette, never held in close-up in daylight | The real chassis models |
| Nothing on the surface moves | Every surface shot has a camera move so the frame is not static; no wide hold on the yard | Animated machines, a hoist that runs |
| The teaching panel is a 2D Python view | Shot over the shoulder at an angle, treated as a screen in the world rather than as the game's UI | The Godot teaching interface |
| Underfoot detail is the weakest scale | No macro floor shots except the wet rail in shot 16, which is the strongest one | The displacement work in `PHOTOREAL.md` |
| The Assayer has never been modelled in Godot | Shots 23–25 are the hardest in the trailer and may have to be built for it | A real Assayer in the cave spike |
| No music | Deliberate, and defensible, but it is a choice and not an accident | A composer |

If shots 23 to 25 cannot be built in time, Act V drops to the fold alone and the trailer runs
1:52. The lie is the stronger beat of the two and the machinery can wait for the next one.
